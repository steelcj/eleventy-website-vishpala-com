## Showing up early

For about three decades, Christopher Steel has made a habit of showing up early to new technology. He worked on the first full-length IMAX 3D animated film, helped build one of North America's first networked digital signage systems, joined an early virtual reality startup, and later ran research computing infrastructure for neuroinformatics and health research at McGill.

Each time, the same questions followed him. Who does this actually serve? How well does it serve them? And how well does it serve our world?

## Accessibility from the start

Those questions led to accessibility work long before it was common: building screen reader compatible websites, founding Voice of Access, a non-profit focused on accessible multilingual web content, and advocating for better public transit access.

## Why Vishpala exists

Vishpala brings that experience together. It builds open source content pipelines that carry material from an archive you own to the websites where your audience finds it, with hosting where you want it.

The work is guided by a few commitments:

- **Serve the people often left out:** older adults, underserved communities, people using older devices, and anyone on a slow or intermittent connection.
- **Protect your agency:** automation that avoids vendor lock-in, and infrastructure that can be migrated over time.
- **Keep it open:** Vishpala's work is published as open source on [GitHub](https://github.com/steelcj).

Based in Verdun, Montréal, Vishpala works with individuals, small businesses, nonprofits and smaller public bodies.
